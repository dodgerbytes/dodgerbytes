The Dodgers aren't expected to hand out a big long-term contract this winter, and that could decide where Tarik Skubal ends up.

## What's being reported

Per **Katie Woo of The Athletic**, the Dodgers' interest in Skubal depends on him being open to a **short-term deal**. Woo suggests the Dodgers aren't likely to make a lucrative long-term offer to anybody this offseason, Skubal or otherwise.

The reason is the labor fight. With a new collective bargaining agreement still being negotiated, and a possible salary cap on the table, the Dodgers may want to avoid long-term commitments until the rules are clear. Woo notes they could still offer a strong average annual value.

## The problem

Per **Jeff Passan of ESPN**, Skubal is targeting a long-term deal and has no interest in a short-term one. Passan also reported the Red Sox and White Sox aren't involved in the bidding.

## Why it matters

The Dodgers have rarely gone long on free agents. Over the past decade they've gone beyond six years only twice, for Shohei Ohtani (10 years) and Yoshinobu Yamamoto (12 years). Kyle Tucker's deal was four years and $240 million with opt-outs. MLB Trade Rumors predicted a nine-year, $425 million deal for Skubal.

Something has to give: a short deal with a huge salary, or Skubal signs somewhere else.
